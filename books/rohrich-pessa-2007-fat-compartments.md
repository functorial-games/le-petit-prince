# Rohrich & Pessa (2007) — *The Fat Compartments of the Face: Anatomy and Clinical Implications for Cosmetic Surgery*

**Reference:** Rod J. Rohrich, Joel E. Pessa, *Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery* 119(7), 2219–2227, 2007. DOI: 10.1097/01.prs.0000265403.66886.54.

**Source used:** PubMed-linked metadata and University of Texas Southwestern abstract/summary.

## Summary

Cadaver dye-injection/dissection experiments supported the view that facial subcutaneous fat is partitioned into distinct compartments rather than forming one confluent layer.

Reported regions include distinct forehead, orbital, cheek/nasolabial, and jowl compartments. Septal boundaries separate neighboring compartments.

## Importance to this repository

This is strong anatomical evidence against modeling the pilot's heavy cheek as merely an outward displacement of one homogeneous skin shell.

For mechanics, compartment boundaries suggest that:
- local volume can change shape without requiring all neighboring fat to shear identically;
- relative motion between compartments may matter;
- cheek, nasolabial, orbital and jowl regions deserve independent geometric representation.

The paper is anatomical/surgical evidence, not a constitutive law. It does not provide validated finite-element stiffness values for those compartments.
